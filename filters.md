# Data Garden Filters

## The evolution of filters

Each pass added a new constraint. The filters kept narrowing until we arrived at something specific.

---

## Filter 1: Irreversibility

Three forms:

### 1. Temporal irreversibility

You captured something that may disappear.

### 2. Transformational irreversibility

You resolved ambiguities and created a canonical representation that wasn't available directly.

### 3. Feedback irreversibility

Real humans/agents used the transformed object, producing trust, corrections and behavioral data.

---

## Filter 2: Transformation difficulty

> **The garden should not merely archive data. It should perform a non-trivial transformation that converts messy reality into a canonical asset.**

If the raw data is already clean, historical, and easily downloadable, elapsed time alone is a weaker moat. Someone can reconstruct most of your history later.

The garden equation:

$$
G \approx
U \times T \times X \times V \times F
$$

Where:

- **U** = uniqueness of source observations
- **T** = time/history
- **X** = difficulty of transformation
- **V** = verification/provenance
- **F** = feedback/use

If **X ≈ 0**, be suspicious.

---

## Filter 3: The canonical rule

> **Do not garden information that already exists as information. Garden latent quantities that must be continuously inferred from fragmented reality.**

Every proposed garden must answer:

> **What difficult canonical object are we manufacturing that does not already exist?**

Not:

> What can we scrape?

---

## Filter 4: The 2030 AGI hindsight test

> It is September 2030. AGI exists. What data do we deeply wish we had been collecting since 2026? What is now entirely useless?

What we'd WISH we had:

- Agent work economics
- Real GPU utilization across the physical economy
- Inference cost decomposition
- Human-AI task boundary migration
- Physical constraint migration
- Model capability trajectory (real-world, not benchmarks)
- AI failure mode taxonomy
- Regulatory and governance response timing
- Software supply chain dependency mapping
- Attention migration across knowledge domains

What is USELESS:

- Raw price data
- Social media metrics
- GitHub stars/forks
- Generic crypto on-chain data
- Headline news feeds
- Basic hardware specs
- Generic benchmark scores
- Country-level economic statistics
- Public company financials
- Weather data

---

## Filter 5: Audience pull

> Would ordinary interested people repeatedly check/watch/share this?

The previous pass overweighted "future research value" and underweighted the most important forcing function: **would people actually want to consume this every week?**

The right garden sits at the intersection of:

$$
\text{scarce data}
\times
\text{hard transformation}
\times
\text{irrecoverable history}
\times
\text{future relevance}
\times
\text{audience pull}
$$

And audience pull matters twice: it creates distribution/trust over time, and it tells us which parts of the dataset actually matter.

---

## Filter 6: Narrative naturalness

Does the underlying system naturally produce:

- winners
- losers
- surprises
- records
- crises
- races

PowPowPow works because people actually want to know:

- What should I mine?
- What's profitable?
- Which coin is exploding?
- Why did hashrate move?
- Which hardware wins?
- Is this network dying?

The underlying data is difficult, but the outputs are almost tabloid-simple.

---

## Filter 7: Decision value

> Does better information from this garden plausibly lead to better economic outcomes for the consumer?

This is probably even more important than broad audience size.

A garden with 10,000 traders/miners who can each make or save money from it may be far better than one with 1 million casual readers.

The audience does not just find PowPowPow interesting. They consume it because the information can improve a decision with monetary consequences.

That is much stronger than "engaging content."

Creates willingness to:

- return frequently
- subscribe to alerts
- pay for better data
- share discoveries
- build agents against the API
- contribute corrections
- trust the historical record

---

## Filter 8: Primitive human curiosity

The latent variable has to map to a primitive human curiosity.

Good primitives:

```text
money
who is winning?
what is cheapest?
what just changed?
what can I do now?
what was promised?
what actually happened?
what is scarce?
what is breaking?
what will affect my job?
what is growing fastest?
```

A perfect garden combines one of these with difficult data.

## The full filter set

| Filter | Question |
|--------|----------|
| **Audience** | Would ordinary interested people repeatedly check/watch/share this? |
| **Narrative** | Does the underlying system naturally produce winners, losers, surprises, records, crises, races? |
| **Transformation** | Are we creating a useful quantity that nobody publishes cleanly? |
| **Irrecoverability** | Will some of the raw evidence/history disappear? |
| **2030 hindsight** | Would we regret not having captured 2026–2030? |
| **Verification** | Does reality eventually tell us whether we were right? |
| **Compounding** | Does usage improve the garden? |
| **Agent utility** | Would future agents prefer querying us rather than rebuilding it? |
| **Simplicity** | Can one person plant the first useful version now? |
| **Joinability** | Does it connect into the larger causal graph? |
| **Decision value** | Does this information help someone allocate capital, reduce cost, avoid loss, or capture opportunity? |
| **Narrative naturalness** | Does the system naturally produce winners, losers, races, surprises? |
| **Primitive curiosity** | Does this map to a question people naturally ask? |

---

## The full garden equation

$$
G =
D \times X \times T \times E \times A \times F
$$

Where:

- **D**: difficult/raw data
- **X**: non-trivial transformation
- **T**: irreversible historical accumulation
- **E**: **economic decision value**
- **A**: audience pull
- **F**: feedback/trust compounding

And if **E is low**, reject it.

## The deepest forcing function

> **Find an important quantity people care about that does not naturally exist as a clean measurement. Build the machinery that continuously manufactures it. Then turn that measurement into a story people habitually follow.**

---

## What the best outputs look like

Not:

> "Here's our dataset."

Instead:

```text
Mining profitability ↓ 31%
Intelligence/$ ↑ 18%
AI labor cost ↓ 22%
Robot labor hours ↑ 140%
AI capacity delivered ↑ 7%
```

Those become **indices people follow**.

And once people follow an index:

```text
measurement
→ media
→ habitual audience
→ citations
→ trust
→ people send us corrections/data
→ better measurement
→ better index
```

That's the complete garden.

## The search criteria

The next brainstorm should specifically search for **other markets where useful economic variables are latent, fragmented, time-sensitive, and currently painful to calculate**.

That is a much tighter problem than "what datasets should we collect?"

---

## The strongest candidates (ranked by all filters)

**PowPowPow** — Where is compute economically flowing in PoW?

**Intelligence Index** — How much useful intelligence does $1 buy?

**AI Labor Index** — What economically useful work can AI perform now?

**Robot Economy** — What useful physical work can machines perform, and at what cost?

**AI Buildout Reality** — How much of the gigantic AI infrastructure boom actually exists?

The progression:

```text
ENERGY/COMPUTE → PowPowPow
INTELLIGENCE → Intelligence Index
DIGITAL LABOR → AI Labor Index
PHYSICAL LABOR → Robot Economy
PHYSICAL CAPACITY → AI Buildout Reality
```

Every one produces a chart people can intuitively understand.

---

## The pattern

The strongest candidates all share:

1. **Everyone wants the answer** (cost of AI work, binding constraint, effective price)
2. **Nobody directly publishes it** (requires multi-source transformation)
3. **It changes constantly** (high temporal irreversibility)
4. **The answer is a latent variable** (must be inferred, not scraped)
5. **By 2030 it will be historically unanswerable** (data disappears)
6. **It maps to a primitive human curiosity** (money, winning, cheapest, scarce, broken)
7. **It leads to better economic decisions** (capital allocation, cost reduction, opportunity capture)

The weakest candidates are all:

- Already published by someone
- Static or slowly changing
- Directly observable
- Low transformation needed
- Reconstructible later
- No clear decision value
- No natural narrative
